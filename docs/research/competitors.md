# Competitor Research

## Market categories

### Vehicle history
Examples researched: HPI, Total Car Check, VCheck, MotorCheck, VehicleScore, carVertical.

Common strength:
- convenient pre-purchase aggregation;
- finance/write-off/theft/salvage/mileage checks depending on provider;
- recognizable consumer reports.

Opportunity:
- most value is concentrated around purchase rather than lifetime ownership;
- reports can disagree because coverage differs;
- missing data can be misread as a clean history;
- government-derived information is sometimes bundled into paid reports despite official free access;
- report/credit expiry creates poor continuity.

### Parts and fitment
TecDoc/TecAlliance demonstrates the scale and value of normalized vehicle-to-part relationships. Rebuilding a global catalogue from scratch is not a sensible v0.1 objective. Treat licensed fitment providers as potential adapters/suppliers.

### Workshop/RMI
Autodata/HaynesPro-style products provide authoritative repair procedures, specifications, diagrams and labour information under commercial licences. Do not copy them. Future licensing can add high-value technical blocks where economics justify it.

### Consumer OBD / AI mechanic
Products such as proprietary scanner/app combinations validate demand for understandable diagnostics. Opportunity: hardware-neutral interpretation, evidence-aware diagnosis, persistent vehicle context and transparent subscription value.

## Positioning

Do not position as “another cheaper history report.”

Position around the full vehicle lifecycle:

**What is this vehicle? → Can I trust the evidence? → What is actually installed? → What will it need? → What is wrong now? → What should I test next? → Did the repair solve it?**

## Competitive moat hypothesis

The moat is not public-record access. It is the structured evidence + component + diagnostic outcome graph accumulated across persistent Vehicle Passports.
